01 · PURPOSE                                                                                  02

Literature Review Goals

• Provide a solid theoretical foundation for the proposed study in the
   dissertation course (related to “what is already known?”)

• To help the student understand the existing body of knowledge including
   where excess research exists (i.e. what is already know?) and where new
   research is needed (i.e. what is needed to be known?)

• Substantiate the presence of the research problem (related to “what is
   needed to be known?”)

• Frame the valid research methodologies, approach, goals, and research
   questions for the proposed study (dissertation)

• In certain cases, justify the proposed study as one that contributes
   something new to the body of knowledge

What is Systematic Literature Review (SLR)?

              A systematic literature review (often referred to as a systematic review) is a
               means of identify, evaluate and interpret available research relevant to a
                particular research question, or topic area, or phenomenon of interest.
               Individual studies contributing to a systematic review are called primary
                         studies; a systematic review is a form of secondary study.

01 · PURPOSE                                                                               02

Reasons for Performing Systematic Literature Review?

• To summarize the existing evidence concerning a treatment or technology
   e.g. to summarize the empirical evidence of the benefits and limitations of a
   specific agile method.

• To identify any gaps in current research in order to suggest areas for further
   investigation.

• To provide a framework/background in order to appropriately position new
   research activities.

    Searching ≠ Screening ≠ Reading ≠ Synthesis

    A literature review is not simply a search for papers: each stage has a different purpose.

                                                                                                 with the RQs.                                                gaps.
    Result: candidate records.                           Result: eligible studies.

11 · AI-ASSISTED REVIEWING

Acceptable vs. Unacceptable Uses of AI

A 5-minute critical exercise: decide whether each use preserves traceability, reproducibility and researcher judgement.

                 ACCEPTABLE — with verification                                                                UNACCEPTABLE / HIGH RISK

✓ Refine RQs and generate candidate synonyms                                               ✕ Ask AI to ‘find all relevant papers’ and stop there
✓ Critique a Boolean search string                                                         ✕ Use references without checking the original source / DOI
✓ Prioritise records for human screening                                                   ✕ Let AI make final inclusion/exclusion decisions autonomously
✓ Draft an extraction matrix from predefined fields                                        ✕ Accept extracted values without checking the paper
✓ Compare evidence already verified in the papers                                          ✕ Claim a research gap only because AI suggested it
✓ Improve wording of a synthesis written from validated evidence                           ✕ Generate the final review without documenting AI use

                                                            Decision test: Can I trace it? Can I verify it? Can I reproduce it? Who made the scientific decision?
